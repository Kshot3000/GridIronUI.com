Bet types, every one explained

Eight ways to bet, from the simplest to the spiciest. Each one comes with a worked example so you can see exactly how it wins, loses, and pays.

Moneyline — who wins, period

The simplest bet in sports: pick the winner. No points, no conditions. Favorites pay less, underdogs pay more.

Worked example. Bills −180 vs. Dolphins +155. A $180 moneyline bet on Buffalo wins $100 if the Bills win by any score. A $100 bet on Miami wins $155. Overtime counts — the bet settles on the final result.

Heavy favorites (e.g. −300) are expensive: you risk a lot to win a little. That's why many bettors prefer spreads or look for underdog value instead of laying big chalk.

Point spread — the great equalizer

The spread handicaps the favorite by a set number of points. The favorite must win by more than the spread ("cover"); the underdog can lose by fewer than the spread — or win outright — and still cash.

Worked example. Eagles −6.5 (−110) vs. Commanders +6.5 (−110). Philadelphia 27, Washington 20: the Eagles won by 7, which is more than 6.5 — Eagles spread bettors win. If the final were 27–21 (a 6-point win), Eagles bettors lose and Commanders +6.5 cashes. If the spread is a whole number (e.g. −7) and the margin lands exactly on it, it's a push — your stake is refunded.

Key numbers matter: in the NFL, games land on 3 and 7 more than any other margins, so −2.5 vs. −3.5 is a much bigger gap than it looks. Line shopping for the best spread is one of the highest-value habits in betting.

Totals (over/under) — the scoreboard, not the winner

Bet on the combined score of both teams going over or under a number set by the book. You don't care who wins.

Worked example. Chiefs–Bills total 48.5 (−110 each side). Final score 30–24 = 54 combined → Over bettors win. 24–20 = 44 → Under wins. A 48.5 line means there's no push — with a whole number like 48, landing exactly on 48 refunds both sides.

Weather is a totals bettor's edge: sustained wind over ~15 mph suppresses scoring in football more than rain does. Check game-day weather before betting outdoor totals.

Parlays — high risk, high reward

Combine multiple bets ("legs") into one wager. Every leg must win. Miss one and the whole ticket loses — but the payout multiplies, because the odds of each leg are multiplied together.

Worked example. Three −110 legs: decimal odds 1.91 × 1.91 × 1.91 = 6.97. A $10 parlay returns about $69.70 ($59.70 profit). The true probability of hitting all three at fair odds is (1/1.91)³ ≈ 14.4% — the payout looks juicy because the event is unlikely.

Honest caveat: parlays carry a much bigger house edge than straight bets, and same-game parlays are priced with extra correlation margin by the book. Fun in small doses; dangerous as a strategy. Run the numbers first in the parlay calculator.

Teasers — buy points, pay a premium

A teaser is a parlay where you get extra points on each spread — typically 6 points in NFL — in exchange for a much shorter payout. All legs must still win.

Worked example. A 6-point NFL teaser: Bills −7.5 becomes −1.5, and Dolphins +1.5 becomes +7.5. A standard 2-team, 6-point teaser pays around −120. Crossing the key numbers of 3 and 7 is where teasers get their value — teasing a −7.5 favorite down through 7 and 3 is the classic sharp teaser leg.

Prop bets — players, not teams

Bet on individual achievements rather than the game outcome: a quarterback's passing yards, a striker's shots on target, whether a player scores a touchdown.

Worked example. "Josh Allen over/under 267.5 passing yards (−115/−105)." If Allen throws for 300 yards, Over cashes regardless of who wins the game. Game props work the same way for team achievements ("Bills to score in every quarter: Yes +400").

Props are where research pays: sportsbooks shade popular-player overs, and limits are lower because books know props are beatable with good projections. Line shop aggressively — a half-yard on a prop line is proportionally huge.

Futures — bet the season

Long-term bets on outcomes decided weeks or months out: championship winners, division winners, season win totals, awards (MVP, Rookie of the Year).

Worked example. "Chiefs to win the Super Bowl +600" in September. A $100 bet wins $600 if Kansas City lifts the trophy in February. Season win totals work like totals: "Bills over/under 10.5 wins" — 11+ wins cashes the Over.

Two honest notes: your money is tied up for months, and futures carry some of the fattest margins in the book (often 20%+ hold). Shop for the best number — futures prices vary wildly between books — and compare with prediction market prices as a reality check.

Live betting — the game is the board

Odds update in real time as the game unfolds. A pre-game −150 favorite that goes down 14–0 might be +120 live — the market reprices every play.

Worked example. You liked the Celtics −4.5 pre-game but didn't bet. They trail by 12 at halftime; the live spread is Celtics +2.5. If you still believe, you now get a far better number — but the market has also seen the same first half you have.

Live lines move fast and the book's margin is wider in-play. Have a plan before kickoff (e.g. "I'll take the favorite live if they fall behind early") rather than impulse-betting every swing.
